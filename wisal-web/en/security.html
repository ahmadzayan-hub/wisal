Security in Wisal

We describe what exists, without unproven promises.

العربية

1) The AI key

  • Android: the Groq key is kept in encrypted storage backed by Android Keystore, and is not included in backups.
  • Windows: settings, including the provider key, are stored in a local JSON file that is not encrypted. Rely on your device's protection and do not share the settings folder.

2) Backups

The backup you export on Android does not contain the Groq key, but it does contain your private data: people, history and preferences. Sharing it shares that data. You are responsible for where you keep it.

3) Updating without losing your data

  • Take a backup first.
  • An Android update must use the same signing certificate and a higher version number than the installed app.
  • Do not uninstall the app to get around a signature mismatch: uninstalling can erase your private data.

4) What Wisal does not do

  • It does not send messages on its own.
  • It does not upload your contacts or collect data from outside accounts. Contact import is optional and reading happens on your device.
  • It has no external analytics tool and does not switch providers automatically.

5) Wisal Direct: still in development

We are building a way to deliver messages directly between two devices. This is not encrypted messaging yet, and it is not available in the app as a finished feature. We will not call it "encrypted" until the implementation and tests actually prove it.

Built so far in the code, and not yet connected to the app you have:

  • A device identity generated on the device, with no phone number, no email and no contact upload.
  • Pairing invitations between two devices, valid for 48 hours and usable once.
  • A delivery server (relay) that stores opaque messages only, with no field for message text, deletes a message as soon as delivery is confirmed, keeps nothing longer than 14 days, and logs no content.
  • Device revocation on the delivery server: a revoked device cannot send or receive, and its id cannot be registered again.

Before any encryption claim, all of these must pass:

  • Tests of pairing two devices, delivery while offline, and delivery acknowledgment.
  • An automated scan that message text never appears in server storage, logs or backups.
  • Tests of device revocation and rejection of expired or reused invitations.
  • An external security review.

Note: a lost or stolen device cannot revoke itself today. That needs a second linked device or a recovery key, which are not built yet.

6) AI and encryption

Generation requests to Groq are not end-to-end encrypted: the provider sees the text needed for generation. Wisal Direct and business mode do not change that.
